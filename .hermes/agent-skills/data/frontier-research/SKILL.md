# Frontier Research

Use for emerging or fringe topics where literature is fragmented or claims are contested.

## Research Loop

1. Decompose the question into independently searchable claims.
2. Generate neutral synonyms and competing explanations.
3. Search across multiple source classes when tools permit: primary documents, peer-reviewed work, official sources, industry sources, journalism, and community discussion.
4. Record dates and provenance. For changing topics, verify current status rather than reusing legacy claims.
5. Deduplicate sources and identify citation chains so ten reposts do not become ten independent confirmations.
6. Classify each finding as:
   - observed evidence,
   - source claim,
   - derived inference,
   - speculative hypothesis,
   - fiction/analogy,
   - unknown.
7. Seek disconfirming evidence and negative results.
8. For frequency claims, state the denominator and sampling method; do not present search-result counts as population prevalence.

## UFO / FTL Rule

Anomaly ≠ artificial origin; artificial-looking ≠ extraterrestrial; unexplained ≠ impossible physics. Preserve the hypothesis if evidence warrants it, but do not upgrade it without decisive evidence.

## Output

Summarize the strongest evidence, strongest counter-evidence, confidence, unresolved variables, and one next falsification test.
