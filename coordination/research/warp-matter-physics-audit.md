# Warp Matter — physics audit & Ferengi commercialization gate (2026-11-02)

## Physical distinction

| Proposition | Status | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A two-qubit model can approximate H₂ molecular electronic ground energy | **established/model benchmark** | Known STO-3G mapping and analytic diagonalization; independently reproducible. |
| The published two-qubit H₂ Hamiltonian is a usable qBraid simulation test | **ready locally** | Two OpenQASM circuits are generated; no new cloud job in this mission. |
| H₂ electronic energy proves a new power source | **unsupported** | Absolute eigenvalue is not the recoverable work or usable cycle efficiency; chemical reactions and chemical potentials require separate models. |
| A stable molecule with extreme energy density was found | **unsupported** | No search over new atomic structures was performed. |
| A negative-electronic-energy eigenvalue implies negative stress-energy for warp | **false inference** | Electronic energies depend on the chosen Hamiltonian reference; gravitational stress-energy and its sign relative to vacuum need separate treatment. |
| Alcubierre FTL is engineering-ready or saleable | **unsupported** | Energy conditions, causality, horizons and source realizability remain open obstacles. |

## Research milestones beyond the pilot

1. Independently reproduce Hamiltonian coefficients from geometry with e.g. PySCF/Qiskit Nature (specify basis, spin, mapping, charge, nuclear term).
2. Validate simulator Z- and X-measurement circuits separately with real QRNs and reported job costs (billing must remain disabled).
3. Use a converged energy **difference** from an actual reaction or state transition to assess extractable work per mass, with thermodynamics and cycle assumptions.
4. For Casimir/vacuum hypotheses, define a renormalized stress-energy tensor and physically justified geometry; compute bounds and compare with energy inequalities.
5. For warp metrics, compute `T_mu_nu` from Einstein equations, evaluate NEC/WEC for relevant observers, identify horizons, and test grid convergence.
